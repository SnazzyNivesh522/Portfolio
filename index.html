Selected work

Production work at SharkStriker, a managed detection and response company, written up like incident reports. Details stay at the architecture level.

Problem. Testers spent most of every engagement on repetitive reconnaissance and vulnerability scanning across 40–60 IP scopes.

What I did. Architected a customer-facing platform that orchestrates more than twenty reconnaissance and scanning tools (Nuclei, OpenVAS, Nmap, ZAP and others) into one automated workflow.

Manual VA and recon effort dropped by about 80%, and the team now spends that time on penetration testing and new vulnerabilities. It earned me the Rising Star award in Q1 2025.

Problem. Servers in each region had their own public IPs and open outbound access, and the cloud address plan had to coexist with a large on-prem and VPN network.

What I did. Redesigned every region around a single controlled ingress, public and private subnets, and restricted egress. Architected the VPC/VCN layout for seven regions on one summarizable cloud supernet that cannot collide with on-prem ranges, and replaced bastion SSH with AWS Systems Manager Session Manager.

A much smaller external attack surface and an address plan that scales to new regions without renumbering.

Problem. A production cluster hit the disk flood-stage watermark. Indices went read-only, Kibana broke, and 962 shards were unassigned. Losing replicas was acceptable; losing indexed data was not.

What I did. Recovered the cluster with zero data loss, then architected disaster recovery and S3-backed searchable snapshots so older data stays queryable from object storage instead of filling primary disks.

The cluster is back to healthy, and the storage pressure that caused the incident is designed out.

Problem. When one of four ISP links went down, DNAT and Cloudflare load balancing on port 443 fell apart for three domains.

What I did. Traced the request path through Cloudflare, each ISP's NAT rules and our routing to find where traffic was being misdirected, then corrected the flow.

HTTPS restored for all three domains, with a clear picture of how failover behaves on every link.

What I did. Implemented TLS/mTLS with an internal CA and certificate renewal, and took HashiCorp Vault from staging to production for the frontend, backend and Python teams. Led a two-phase migration off WHMCS to a React-based architecture authenticated through Keycloak and Microsoft Entra External ID, working around the identity providers' lack of per-user redirect URIs with a shared callback and OAuth state routing.

Credentials and certificates now come from one managed system instead of being scattered across services.

What I did. Spearheaded the company-wide move from manual deployments to a self-hosted GitLab workflow, and wrote the documentation and training on Git, branching, versioning and CI/CD for developers at every level. Separately, consolidated several standalone FastAPI and Flask services into a single OpenAPI contract with change notifications for frontend consumers.

Every team now ships through versioned pipelines instead of copying files to servers.

Vulnerability intelligence. Daily CVE sync from NVD and MITRE, enriched with CWE, CAPEC, ATT&CK and D3FEND and mapped to ISO 27001, NIST CSF, SOC 2 and NIST SP 800-53.

Brand protection. Detects cybersquatting and impersonation domains from homoglyph, keyboard, typo, TLD and dictionary mutations, scored with WHOIS, DNS and infrastructure fingerprints.

What I did. Built a classification service over 900GB+ in S3, about 6 million files including 5.3 million images.

Flagged roughly 100,000 policy-violating images and escalated about 3,600 high-risk items to priority human review.
